## Purpose

This document states the collective agreement for how [ORGANIZATION] governs itself, and how each
person acting on its behalf conducts themselves with each other, with those the organization
serves, and with the world.

A code of ethics is not decoration. Its function is to settle, in advance and in calm conditions,
what the organization will do when a decision is commercially attractive and ethically wrong. Those
decisions arrive under time pressure, with incomplete information, and usually with someone senior
wanting an answer. Deciding the principle beforehand is the only reliable way to hold it.

The analogy is an advance medical directive: the point is not that you expect the bad situation, it
is that your judgment in the moment will be worse than your judgment now, and you are choosing to
be bound by the better one.

The items here do not change frequently. They are foundational, and they shape everything
downstream of them.

## Part 1 — Where We Are Going

### 1.1 First principles

> `[SOURCE GAP]` — State the small number of things your organization holds to be true, that sit
> beneath your values and do not change.

First principles are more than a declaration of values. They are the statements you treat as
universally true, which then shape what you value, how you work, and how you decide. Most
organizations have between one and three. If you have ten, you have written values, not principles.

**Record for each:** the statement itself, and what follows from it that would not follow otherwise.

### 1.2 Vision, mission, and long-range goal

> `[SOURCE GAP]` — Complete from your own strategic planning. See
> [LINK: Strategic Planning and Vision Setting] for the method.

| Element | What it answers | Note |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Vision** | What does the world look like if we succeed? | Describes the world, not the company |
| **Mission** | What do we do about it, every day? | Should be recognizable to a new hire in week one |
| **Long-range goal** | What specific, measurable outcome by what date? | A number and a year, not an aspiration |
| **How we do this** | The mechanism | The part competitors cannot copy from your website |

Record when each was set, and who was in the room. A statement whose provenance is unknown is one
nobody feels ownership of.

### 1.3 Guiding values

> `[SOURCE GAP]` — This section must be completed by your organization. It cannot be templated,
> because values that are inherited rather than chosen do not survive contact with a hard decision.

State between three and six values. For each, record:

| Element | Why it is required |
| --- | --- |
| **The value** | A single word or short phrase |
| **What it means here** | The same word means different things at different organizations. Define yours. |
| **What it looks like in practice** | Two or three observable behaviors. If you cannot name a behavior, it is not a value. |
| **What it costs** | The decision this value will make you decline. A value with no cost is a preference. |

The last row is the one that matters. A stated value that has never caused the organization to turn
down revenue, delay a launch, or part company with a high performer is not yet operative.

**Requirements:**

* Values **MUST** be approved by [ROLE: Board or Governing Body] and reviewed at least annually.
* Values **MUST** be stated to all personnel, and **SHOULD** be stated publicly.
* Where a decision is made contrary to a stated value, the reasoning **MUST** be recorded.

## Part 2 — Guiding Principles

These translate the values into conduct. Adapt the headings to your own values; the structure —
principle, then the questions that operationalize it — is what carries across.

### 2.1 Build and protect trust

The success of the work relies on trust. Holding a space where people can be honest requires that
they trust the organization, that colleagues trust each other, and that each person trusts
themselves.

Trust rests on four pillars:

| Pillar | What it commits us to |
| --- | --- |
| **Integrity** | Clear quality requirements, honest sharing, listening with real attention |
| **Responsibility** | Each person owns their conduct, their actions, and the outcomes of those actions |
| **Forgiveness** | Experimentation and failure are invited; learning is the expected response to error |
| **Compassion** | People are met where they are, including when they have got it wrong |

**Questions to ask before an action that could affect trust:**

* Am I being honest with myself about how I am showing up to this situation?
* Does this action honor my integrity, or the integrity of the work, the organization, and our
  stakeholders?
* Am I owning my actions, past and present, that are contributing to this situation?
* Am I approaching this with compassion, in pursuit of understanding rather than of being right?
* Does this action support the growth of the people involved?

A strong foundation of trust is built by being able to answer yes, and by supporting each other to
act accordingly.

### 2.2 When we measure results, humanize the numbers

Data informs decisions and is a powerful tool. It has also, repeatedly, been treated as truth with
harmful results. Data as we generally encounter it compresses complicated human situations into one
or two dimensions. That compression is useful and it is lossy, and forgetting the second part is
where the damage occurs.

**Before collecting, stewarding, using, or sharing data, consider:**

* Does this data humanize or dehumanize the people it describes?
* What bias is potentially inherent in how it is collected, interpreted, and presented?
* Why are we collecting and using it? How, and to what end?
* Will its use support or hinder the people it describes?
* Are we stewarding it securely?
* Does its collection or use honor privacy, anonymity, and confidentiality?

**Requirements:**

* Personnel **MUST** handle data in line with the Data Classification and Handling Policy and the
  Data Collection Standard.
* Decisions materially affecting people **MUST NOT** rest solely on an automated or statistical
  output without human review.

<!-- This section is where an ethics policy either does real work or does none. Most data harm is done by people following a metric in good faith, not by people acting badly. -->

### 2.3 Govern and operate with transparency, openness, and representation

**Requirements:**

* [ORGANIZATION] **MUST** be transparent with personnel about how it operates as a business.
* Personnel **MAY** approach [ROLE: Executive Sponsor] for detail on any aspect of the
  organization's operations, and **SHOULD** expect a substantive answer.
* Where information cannot be shared, the reason **SHOULD** be given, rather than the request
  going unanswered.

### 2.4 Identifying and addressing friction and conflict

Friction and conflict arise — with ourselves, with each other, with those we serve, and with the
world. Personnel may also face situations where acting on the organization's behalf conflicts with
their own principles or interests.

Conflict is addressed through transparency (naming it), openness (talking about it), and trust.
Avoiding it does not resolve it; it defers it at interest.

**Requirements:**

* Personnel **MUST** raise friction that affects the work rather than absorbing it.
* Where acting on [ORGANIZATION]'s behalf would conflict with a person's own principles, they
  **MUST** be able to say so to [ROLE: Executive Sponsor] without penalty.
* Questions about how to handle a conflict go to [ROLE: People/HR Lead] or
  [ROLE: Executive Sponsor].

## Part 3 — Standards of Conduct

### 3.1 Required conduct

All personnel **MUST**:

* Act honestly. Do not misrepresent facts, results, credentials, or capabilities to colleagues,
  customers, regulators, investors, or the public.
* Comply with applicable law and with [ORGANIZATION] policy.
* Protect information entrusted to the organization, in line with the Data Classification and
  Handling Policy, including after employment ends.
* Treat colleagues, customers, and partners with dignity. Harassment, discrimination, and
  retaliation are prohibited without exception.
* Use organizational resources for organizational purposes, in line with the Acceptable Use Policy.
* Record transactions and results accurately and completely.

All personnel **MUST NOT**:

* Offer, give, solicit, or accept anything of value intended to improperly influence a decision.
* Use non-public information obtained through their role for personal benefit, or provide it to
  others for theirs.
* Enter arrangements that commit [ORGANIZATION] without the authority to do so.
* Instruct, pressure, or permit another person to do anything prohibited here.

### 3.2 Conflicts of interest

A conflict of interest exists where a person's private interest could reasonably be seen to
influence their judgment on [ORGANIZATION]'s behalf. The test is *reasonable appearance*, not
proven influence — the standard is deliberately lower than proof, because the damage is to trust,
and trust responds to appearance.

**Circumstances that require disclosure include:**

* Being employed by, or acting as a consultant to, a competitor, potential competitor, supplier, or
  contractor — whether the person or a close family member, and regardless of the nature of the
  employment.
* Hiring or supervising family members or closely related persons.
* Serving as a board member of an outside commercial company or organization.
* Owning or holding a substantial interest in a competitor, supplier, or contractor.
* Holding a personal or financial interest, or a potential gain, in any [ORGANIZATION] transaction.
* Placing [ORGANIZATION] business with a firm owned or controlled by a member of personnel or their
  family.
* Accepting gifts, discounts, favors, or services from a customer, potential customer, competitor,
  or supplier, unless equally available to all personnel or below [TARGET] in value.

**Requirements:**

* Personnel **MUST** disclose actual, potential, and apparent conflicts to [ROLE: Legal Counsel] on
  becoming aware of them, and at least annually.
* Disclosed conflicts **MUST** be recorded in [LINK: Conflict of Interest Register].
* A person with a disclosed conflict **MUST NOT** participate in the related decision unless
  [ROLE: Executive Sponsor] documents why their participation is appropriate.

<!-- tune to your organization: [TARGET] is commonly a per-gift value between $50 and $250. Set a number and apply it consistently. -->

### 3.3 Raising a concern

**Requirements:**

* Any person **MAY** report a suspected violation to their manager, to [ROLE: People/HR Lead], or
  to [ROLE: Legal Counsel]. Where the concern involves those roles, it **MAY** be reported directly
  to [ROLE: Board or Governing Body].
* Reports **MAY** be made anonymously via [LINK: Reporting Channel].
* Good-faith reports **MUST NOT** result in retaliation, regardless of whether the concern is
  substantiated. Retaliation is itself a violation of this policy.
* [ROLE: People/HR Lead] **MUST** acknowledge a report within [TARGET] business days and **MUST**
  record its disposition.
* Knowingly false reports made in bad faith are themselves a violation.

The obligation is to report, not to investigate. Personnel are not expected to establish that a
violation occurred before raising it.

### 3.4 Consequences

Violations are handled under [LINK: Disciplinary Procedure] and may result in action up to and
including termination and referral to authorities. Consequences apply irrespective of the
violator's seniority, tenure, or performance.

> Adapt or remove. Included because a code of ethics that reads only as prohibition tends to be
> received as suspicion, and a closing statement of intent costs nothing and changes the tone of
> everything above it.

with how we work. Our intention in working with you, and with those we serve, is that it be
mutually beneficial. If you are not experiencing that, we want to know — reach out to
