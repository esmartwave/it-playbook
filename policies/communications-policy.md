## Purpose

The purpose of this policy is to set [ORGANIZATION]'s expectations for how people communicate with
each other, with those the organization serves, and with the world.

Communication is where most operational failures become visible and where many of them begin. A
change that is technically sound but poorly communicated is experienced as a disruption. A concern
raised through the wrong channel is experienced as a complaint. This policy exists to make the
default path the effective one, so that good intent is not lost in delivery.

## Scope

This policy applies to:

* **All personnel** — employees, contractors, and third parties acting on [ORGANIZATION]'s behalf.
* **All channels** — in person, live virtual meetings, recorded or asynchronous material, chat,
  email, written correspondence, published articles, and social media.
* **All audiences** — colleagues, customers or those served, partners, vendors, and the public.

This policy governs *how* people communicate. It does not govern what may be disclosed — see the
Data Classification and Handling Policy for that.

## Policy

### 1. Communication is an exchange, not a transmission

A message is not communicated because it was sent. It is communicated when it is received and
understood. Personnel are expected to confirm understanding rather than assume it, particularly
when the message carries a change, a decision, or a risk.

Communication may travel up, down, and sideways. In every direction it is treated as a mutual
exchange of verbal and non-verbal meaning, not as a broadcast.

**Requirements:**

* Communications that announce a change **MUST** state what is changing, why, who is affected, and
  what the recipient needs to do.
* Communications that request action **MUST** state the action and the date it is needed by.
* The sender **SHOULD** provide a route for questions, and **SHOULD** treat repeated questions as
  evidence that the message needs rewriting, not repeating.

### 2. Information is not communication

Information is what is shared. Communication is what is understood. Information shared without
context frequently lacks what the recipient needs to understand its impact on them — which is the
part they actually care about.

**Requirements:**

* Where information carries consequence for the recipient, the sender **MUST** supplement it with
  a two-way interaction — a conversation, a meeting, or an open question period.
* The sender **SHOULD** match the format to the audience. Some audiences are well served by a
  written summary; others need a live discussion; others need a one-to-one. The same content in the
  wrong format is not the same communication.

### 3. Match the channel to the message

Each channel carries an implicit signal about urgency and permanence. Using the wrong one distorts
the message regardless of its content.

| Message type | Channel | Rationale |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Urgent, time-bound, action required | [SYNCHRONOUS CHANNEL] plus written follow-up | Needs immediate attention and a durable record |
| Routine operational update | [ASYNCHRONOUS CHANNEL] | Respects focus time; searchable later |
| Decision or commitment | [SYSTEM OF RECORD] | Must be findable by people not in the original conversation |
| Sensitive, personal, or corrective | Direct conversation, then written summary | Tone does not survive text; the record still matters |
| Public or on behalf of the organization | [APPROVED EXTERNAL CHANNEL], per §7 | Carries organizational liability |

<!-- tune to your organization: replace the bracketed channel classes with your actual tools. Define the classes first, then name the tools, so the table survives a tool migration. -->

**Requirements:**

* Decisions **MUST** be recorded in [SYSTEM OF RECORD], not left only in chat or a meeting.
* Sensitive or corrective conversations **MUST NOT** be initiated over chat or in public channels.
* Personnel **MUST NOT** use personal accounts or unmanaged channels for organizational business.

### 4. Consider yourself and your audience

Before communicating, the sender considers:

* What is my mindset? Am I present, and aware of what may be influencing how I communicate?
* How do I feel? Am I in a position to communicate well right now, or should this wait?
* Who am I communicating with, and am I using an approach they will actually receive?
* Am I meeting people in their language, in a way that supports their hearing what I am sharing?
* Are there sensitivities in play, for me or for the audience, that should shape how or when I
  raise this?

Technical and process changes are logical; their effect on people is not. A change that is trivial
to implement may be significant to the person whose daily work it alters. Anticipating that
reaction is part of communicating the change, not a courtesy added afterward.

### 5. Consider the power of words and timing

* Personnel in a position of trust are understood to be guiding, not merely informing.
* Words carry weight across more than the practical dimension of a person's work. They reach
  tangible skills and interactions, emotional state and sense of past and future self, and for some
  people a deeper personal or spiritual dimension. <!-- tune to your organization: adapt or remove this framing to fit your context; some organizations state it explicitly, others do not. -->
* Communicating before an audience is willing or able to receive the message rarely achieves the
  intended result. Timing is part of the message.

### 6. Consider the confidentiality of what you intend to share

Work that involves guiding people or teams often involves receiving information shared in moments
of vulnerability. Honoring that trust means weighing, before sharing, whether a communication
respects the confidence in which something was given.

**Requirements:**

* Personnel **MUST** consult [ROLE: Executive Sponsor] where the confidentiality of information
  they hold is unclear. Uncertainty is a reason to ask, not a reason to proceed.
* Personnel **MUST NOT** disclose information classified above [CLASSIFICATION] outside its
  intended audience. See the Data Classification and Handling Policy.

### 7. Communicating externally

**Requirements:**

* Only personnel authorized by [ROLE: Communications Lead] **MAY** make public statements on
  [ORGANIZATION]'s behalf.
* Interactions with key contacts at organizations [ORGANIZATION] serves **MUST** be coordinated
  with [ROLE: Executive Sponsor], so that the organization speaks to a client with one voice.
* Where personnel identify their employer publicly, they **SHOULD** make clear that views expressed
  are their own.
* Media, regulatory, or legal inquiries **MUST** be routed to [ROLE: Communications Lead]
  unanswered. This includes inquiries that appear routine.
* Communications during an active incident are governed by the Incident Response Plan, which takes
  precedence over this section.

### 8. Feedback is essential; encourage it and address it quickly

Feedback is the information that lets an organization evolve deliberately rather than by accident.
Without it, the cost and risk of delivering the intended result rise sharply, because there is no
signal telling you that you have drifted.

**Requirements:**

* Personnel **MUST** be able to say what is on their minds without retribution. Retaliation for
  good-faith feedback is a violation of this policy and of the Code of Ethics Policy.
* [ORGANIZATION] **MUST** publish how to give feedback, how it is processed, and the expected
  response timeframe. <!-- [SOURCE GAP] — document your actual mechanisms and timeframe here. -->
* [ROLE: People/HR Lead] **MUST** acknowledge feedback within [TARGET] business days.
* [ORGANIZATION] **MUST** act on feedback, either through clarifying communication or through
  relevant action. Receiving feedback without response teaches people to stop giving it.
* Where feedback may prompt a strong reaction, the giver **SHOULD** allow time — during or
  immediately after — for discussion and questions in both directions.
* Communicators at every level **SHOULD** actively seek feedback rather than wait for it.

<!-- tune to your organization: [TARGET] is commonly 2–5 business days. Set a number you can meet. -->

### 9. Support each other to communicate well

**Requirements:**

* Training in effective communication **MUST** be available to team leaders, supervisors, and
  managers.
* [ROLE: Communications Lead] **SHOULD** provide supporting materials to managers ahead of
  significant organizational communications, so the message is consistent when it is repeated.

### 10. Communicate wins, celebrations, friction, and failures

[ORGANIZATION] commits to communicating what it does well and what it needs to improve, quickly,
and in advance where possible.

**Requirements:**

* Rumors **MUST** be addressed with direct communication as soon as practicable. A rumor is
  evidence that a communication gap exists and is being filled by something else.
* Communicating on a "need to know" basis, avoiding controversial issues, or delaying
  communication "until all the details are clear" are **contrary to this policy**.

<!-- The final bullet is the one people will test. Delay is the most common failure mode, and it is always locally reasonable — which is why the policy names it explicitly rather than leaving it to judgment. -->

### 11. Assessing whether communication worked

Effectiveness is assessed, not assumed. The sender **SHOULD** ask:

* Upon reflection, did the communication convey the intended message?
* Can the recipient reflect the intended message back?
* How do we feel about what we communicated?
* Did the intended action follow?
* Is the volume of follow-up questions proportionate to the complexity of the message?
* Is the communication in line with our values and the principles in this policy?

[ROLE: Communications Lead] **SHOULD** review these signals after major communications and record
what to do differently.

## Terms

| Term | Definition |
| --- | --- |
| **Channel** | A medium through which a message travels — chat, email, meeting, published post |
| **System of record** | The authoritative store for a given class of information, where a later reader is expected to look |
| **Synchronous** | Communication where parties participate at the same time |
| **Asynchronous** | Communication the recipient engages with on their own schedule |
| **Information** | What is shared |
| **Communication** | What is mutually understood as a result |
| **Retaliation** | Adverse treatment of a person because they raised a concern or gave feedback |
